Mistakes that cost families when they claim

Patterns that come up again and again in our research library.

⚠️ Buying bank-sold group insurance

If the tie-up between your bank and the insurer ends, your policy gets cancelled and can't be ported, leaving you uninsured in older age.

⚠️ Accepting room-rent limits

Choosing a room above your daily cap triggers proportionate deductions across the whole hospital bill, leaving you with big out-of-pocket payments.

⚠️ Adding elderly parents to a family floater

The premium for everyone gets calculated at the senior citizen's rate, so your total family cost rises unnecessarily.

⚠️ Relying on a regular top-up instead of a super top-up

A regular top-up pays only when a single bill crosses your deductible, so it pays nothing for several smaller hospital stays in the same year.

⚠️ Choosing a policy with treatment-wise limits

Payout caps on specific treatments leave you paying the balance yourself, even when your total cover is large.

⚠️ Picking a cashless-only plan without reimbursement

If you're admitted to a hospital outside the network in an emergency, the insurer can refuse to cover the treatment.

Myths, and what’s actually true

“My employer cover is enough for my family”

Sach: Company insurance ends as soon as you leave or lose your job, leaving you unprotected when emergencies strike.

“The cheapest premium is the best deal”

Sach: Low premiums often hide room-rent limits, co-payments and treatment caps that make you pay out of pocket.

“I can hide past health issues to lower the premium”

Sach: Hiding existing conditions or medical history leads to claim rejection and policy cancellation for non-disclosure.

“All day-care procedures are covered automatically”

Sach: Without explicit day-care cover, a policy may require a 24-hour hospital stay and reject modern same-day procedures.

“A high cover amount means every cost is paid”

Sach: Policies often exclude hospital consumables and apply deductions unless your contract explicitly protects you.

Questions people ask

How much health cover should my family buy?

Families in big cities should hold a substantial base cover, ideally paired with a low-cost super top-up to handle rising medical costs.

Can I include my elderly parents in our family floater plan?

It's usually better to buy separate policies for parents, so their age doesn't raise the premium for younger family members.

What is a super top-up and how does it save money?

A super top-up pays hospital costs above your base policy limit across the whole year, giving large cover at a low premium.

Why do cashless claims get rejected?

Mainly because of undisclosed past illnesses, admission to a non-network hospital on a cashless-only policy, or a waiting period that hasn't ended.
