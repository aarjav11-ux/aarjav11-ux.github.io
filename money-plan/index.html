Mistakes that cost people years

Patterns that come up again and again in our research library.

⚠️ Investing Before Protecting

Putting money into markets without emergency reserves and pure insurance forces you to sell assets at a loss during a crisis

⚠️ Chasing Past Return Charts

Pouring savings into last year's top-performing funds usually leads to poor results because swelling fund sizes break the strategy

⚠️ Rolling Over Credit Card Dues

Paying only the minimum due triggers annual interest rates of 40% to 50% that rapidly compound into a debt trap

⚠️ Buying Hybrid Insurance-Investment Plans

Opting for products that mix life insurance with returns delivers yields worse than a basic savings account along with poor cover

⚠️ Taking Leveraged or Depreciating Loans

Borrowing for consumer gadgets or taking market leverage risks sudden personal bankruptcy when income or market conditions wobble

⚠️ Trying to Time the Market Bottom

Sitting on idle cash waiting for the absolute bottom risks missing the market's explosive recovery days that generate most long-term wealth

Myths, and what’s actually true

“You need complex calculations and exotic products to create wealth”

Sach: Sticking to plain vanilla mutual funds, emergency deposits, and boring broad-market equities beats complicated schemes

“Buying penny stocks is an easy shortcut to get rich quick”

Sach: Low share prices almost always reflect fundamentally broken businesses rather than bargain opportunities

“Active trading in futures and options is a reliable way to make regular income”

Sach: Over 90% of retail derivatives traders lose their capital while paying heavy costs

“A temporary market correction means your money has drowned permanently”

Sach: Paper drawdowns are not actual losses unless you panic and sell your assets at depressed levels

“You should buy a whole-life insurance cover that lasts until age 85 or”

Sach: You only need term cover until your working years end and dependents become financially independent

“A bigger salary or two incomes will automatically fix all your savings problems”

Sach: Without strict spending rules, lifestyle inflation and upgraded desires will expand just as fast as higher pay

Questions people ask

How much money should I keep aside for emergencies?

You should hold three to six months of essential household expenses in an auto-sweep bank account or safe liquid fund

How much life insurance cover do I actually need?

You should buy a pure term insurance policy equal to 15 to 20 times your annual income, covering only the active earning members

What portion of my monthly income should go into investments?

Aim to invest at least 20% of your earnings right after payday before spending on discretionary desires

How do I decide how much to put into stocks versus safer assets?

Subtract your current age from 100 to determine the baseline percentage to keep in equities, leaving the rest for stability

How much of my take-home income can safely go toward loan EMIs?

Your total monthly EMIs across all loans combined should not exceed 35% to 40% of your net salary

When should I choose fixed deposits over equity mutual funds?

Keep money in fixed deposits for any non-negotiable expense coming up within one to three years to protect your capital from market swings
