Mistakes that cost people years

Patterns that come up again and again in our research library.

⚠️ Chasing Past Returns

Buying schemes solely based on recent one-year or three-year chart-topping performance leads to heavy losses because market cycles rotate and past winners rarely stay on top.

⚠️ Small-Cap Overload

Allocating all your money into high-risk small-cap schemes exposes you to brutal thirty to forty percent drawdowns that trigger panic selling.

⚠️ Ignoring Inflation

Keeping your monthly investment amount flat over decades leaves you with a final corpus worth only a fraction of your goal in real purchasing power.

⚠️ Panic Selling in Crashes

Stopping contributions or redeeming units when the market drops locks in temporary paper losses and robs you of buying at discounted valuations.

⚠️ Investing Without a Safety Net

Starting equity investments without an emergency cash cushion and personal health insurance forces you to break long-term investments prematurely during personal crises.

⚠️ Portfolio Overlap and Clutter

Holding dozens of mutual funds creates administrative confusion and duplicates the exact same underlying company shares without reducing risk.

Myths, and what’s actually true

“A fund with a ten-rupee unit price is cheaper and offers higher upside”

Sach: The unit price is simply the per-unit mathematical division of total assets and has zero bearing on whether a fund is cheap or capable of generating returns.

“Holding ten to fifteen different mutual funds gives you maximum diversification”

Sach: Holding more than five to eight schemes merely duplicates identical company stocks across multiple fund houses without delivering better returns.

“Managing your portfolio completely alone through direct plans is always the best choice”

Sach: Self-directed investors historically stop their investments over two times faster during market crashes because they lack behavioral guidance to prevent panic exits.

“Employer health insurance is all the protection your investment portfolio needs”

Sach: Corporate cover vanishes the moment you switch or lose your job, leaving your mutual fund savings completely exposed to unexpected hospital bills.

Questions people ask

How much money do I need to invest to get dedicated portfolio help?

Personal portfolio management typically requires a minimum five lakh lump sum or a twenty-five thousand monthly investment, though curated fund baskets are available starting at five thousand per month.

What fees or commissions are charged for portfolio management?

Assisted distribution earns an ongoing commission of roughly zero point six percent directly from regular fund investments with no separate upfront advisory bills.

How many mutual funds should I realistically hold in my portfolio?

A well-structured portfolio needs only three to five funds, or at most six to eight schemes, to achieve optimal diversification without clutter.

How often should my mutual fund portfolio be reviewed and rebalanced?

A thorough review should be conducted once a year or whenever market rallies cause your equity allocation to drift significantly from your target.

Do I need to open a stock broker demat account to invest in mutual funds?

No, holding mutual funds in statement of account format through official registrars is completely free and avoids annual demat maintenance charges and platform trading risks.
