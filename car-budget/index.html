Mistakes that cost people years

Patterns that come up again and again in our research library.

⚠️ Stretching budget for social show-off

Buyers stretch a 10 Lakh budget to 20 Lakh just to impress neighbors and relatives who will never pay a single monthly EMI.

⚠️ Calculating only the loan EMI

Planning solely around the monthly bank installment ignores fuel, service, and insurance bills that routinely equal or exceed the EMI itself.

⚠️ Taking 7-year loans to lower payments

Stretching repayment over seven years creates an exhausting debt trap that feels like twenty years while compounding massive interest on a depreciating car.

⚠️ Borrowing heavily for top trims

Taking on extra loan principal for cosmetic features like sunroofs inflates road tax, registration, and interest charges by lakhs of rupees.

⚠️ Draining entire bank savings for down payment

Emptying bank accounts leaves families completely unprotected when sudden household or medical emergencies arise.

⚠️ Buying thirsty petrol cars on tight budgets

Choosing inefficient petrol engines leads to heavy financial anxiety where owners park the car at home or switch off the air conditioner to save money.

Myths, and what’s actually true

“A bank loan approval defines your car budget”

Sach: The bank approves large loans purely to earn interest, whereas your real budget is strictly the liquid cash down payment you can spare.

“A company car lease is free tax-saving money”

Sach: Corporate car leases act as an employer loan trap that locks you into your job for five to six years and halts your professional growth.

“Low daily city mileage means petrol fuel costs will not hurt”

Sach: Short city trips keep engines cold and drop real-world mileage down to single digits, making monthly fuel bills sting heavily.

“Top-spec trims protect your money better through higher resale value”

Sach: Top variants suffer the steepest net rupee depreciation due to inflated upfront taxes and interest charges that secondary buyers never repay.

“Buying an electric vehicle is always a smart way to save money”

Sach: Spending several lakhs extra upfront on an EV when you drive very few kilometers wastes capital that fuel savings can never recover.

“Installing aftermarket accessories voids your manufacturer warranty”

Sach: Dealerships use warranty threats as scare tactics to sell basic electronics at four times their fair market price.

Questions people ask

Can I buy a new car if my monthly income is below ₹1 Lakh?

The sources advise earning at least ₹1 Lakh per month with solid savings before buying any new car to avoid compromising family essentials.

How many years should my car loan tenure be?

The ideal loan tenure is 2 to 3 years, stretching to a maximum of 5 years only if keeping the car long term, while avoiding 7-year traps.

How much liquid cash should I have in savings before buying?

You should ideally hold liquid savings equal to or greater than the full value of the vehicle, retaining an untouched emergency fund.

Should freelancers and self-employed buyers take a car loan?

Freelancers with fluctuating earnings should avoid EMI debt entirely and purchase vehicles via full upfront cash or buy pre-owned.

How do I know if I can truly afford my car day-to-day?

You can afford it if you can fill 20 to 25 liters of fuel at a pump without feeling financial stress or cutting household budgets.
